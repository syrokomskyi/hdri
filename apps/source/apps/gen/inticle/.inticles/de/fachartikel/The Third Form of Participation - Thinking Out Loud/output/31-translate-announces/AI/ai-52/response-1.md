In the startup environment, almost everything revolves around two participation models: salary or equity. But for part of digital work, this is no longer sufficient as an explanation. Especially when a person creates not just task output, but a result that can continue to exist as a separate asset within the product or the studio infrastructure.

- It seems productive to me to distinguish between the created artifact and impact over time. An artifact is a system, library, methodology, or another completed digital object with boundaries, acceptance criteria, and reuse potential. Impact over time is sales, support, operational involvement, engagement, commercial enablement. For startups, this matters because both types of contribution are often merged into the single word “value.”
- If we think in startup terms, participation through what has been created looks like an attempt to build a third channel of entry: not through salary and not through upfront money, but through a fixed contribution. But this immediately raises hard questions: who controls go-to-market, how reporting is structured, what happens if there are no sales, whether the Author can reclaim the created asset, and how to avoid a situation in which the risk is transferred almost entirely to the person creating it.
- In the DACH context, there is also legal sensitivity. If the model is structured carelessly, it can look like a hybrid between collaboration, concealed dependency, and deferred payment without sufficient guarantees. That is why the design of such a structure matters no less than its economic attractiveness.

Do you see, for DACH startups, a realistic third participation format based on what has been created, one that does not collapse back into either salary or classic equity?
