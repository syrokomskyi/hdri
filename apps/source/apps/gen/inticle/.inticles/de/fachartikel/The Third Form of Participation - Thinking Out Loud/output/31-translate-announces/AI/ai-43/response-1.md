In ecommerce, people usually talk about margin, channels, retention, and operational efficiency. But there is another, less obvious issue: how the participants in value creation can take part in that value at all. Usually, only two options remain — salary or capital. I am interested in whether there is room for a third approach: participation through a created digital asset.

- In the DACH context, this is especially interesting where someone creates not just a service, but a reusable output: a component system, an audit methodology, a module, a catalog structure, or another formalized artifact that outlives a single project. Such a contribution can be described, accepted against criteria, and theoretically linked to long-term economic benefit. This is a different logic from simply paying for time.
- But there are also hard constraints. If the value depends on paid traffic, the store’s brand, logistics, the support team, seasonality, and commercial infrastructure, then the contribution of an individual Author becomes highly dependent on a system they do not control. This is precisely where any participation model based on what was created may turn out to be asymmetrical, even if it looks fair in formal terms.
- For me, the key question is not whether such a model is possible at all, but which safeguards make it fair: transparent reporting, the right to reclaim the artifact, clear buyout terms, a defined term for the commercial trial, and understandable boundaries between the asset and the effect. Without this, the structure is vulnerable both economically and morally.

Do you see a realistic scenario in the DACH ecommerce environment where participation through a created digital asset could work better than conventional employment or one-off payment?
