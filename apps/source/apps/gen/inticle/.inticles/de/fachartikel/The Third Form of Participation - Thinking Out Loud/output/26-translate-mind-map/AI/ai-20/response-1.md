# The Third Form of Participation: Thinking Out Loud

I run a web studio in Baden-Württemberg. I am experimenting with a form of participation that fits neither into wages nor into capital.

This is not a finished model. Not a universal solution. It is an attempt to find a way for labor not to disappear together with the end of a project at a time when AI is beginning to displace professions.

## Two forms that dominate

In the modern world, I see two most familiar forms of participation in value creation.

**Wages.** A person sells time. They have no stake in what was created. Safe in the moment, but replaceable. When AI displaces professions, the wage form feels the strongest pressure. It pays for presence. Presence is something a machine can reproduce more cheaply.

**Capital.** A person has a stake. Typical access requires money upfront. Fintech has widened access—fractional shares, mutual funds, crowdinvesting. But this is still participation through money, not through labor. A person without capital remains outside.

Why not look, alongside them, for room for a third form?

**Participation through what is created, not what is bought.** A stake arises from what a person has made. Verifiably, immutably, under their name. Without the need to buy entry.

This echoes older principles: a composer’s royalty, a scientist’s attribution, a craftsperson’s name on the object. A composer receives something from each performance—not because they bought a stake, but because they wrote it. I am trying to extend this principle to labor that previously had no royalties: development, research, operational work.

## Werk and Wirkung: two natures of contribution

In this model, I distinguish between two natures of contribution. Two types of value with different mechanics.

**Werk — that which has state.** A product, asset, object with coordinates and integrity. Verifiable in the moment: “here it is, in this state, with my signature.” Werk accumulates. A design system continues to live and generate value after its creation.

In German copyright law, there is a logic of fair participation by the author in the success of a work. If a Werk truly is a protected work, a stake in it may have legal support. But not every Werkstück is automatically a work in the legal sense. That has to be checked separately.

**Wirkung — that which has effect over time.** Facilitation, influence, flow. Sales, client acquisition, infrastructure support. Verifiable only through dynamics: “here is what changed over a period.” Wirkung fades. The effect dissipates. A client once brought in remains years later because of the studio, not because of the person who once brought them in.

Wirkung does not create a protected work. A stake in Wirkung cannot rest on copyright. This is not a disadvantage. It is simply the truth about the nature of the contribution: where there is no accumulation, it is harder to justify an eternal stake.

## Flow, not stock

What should be measured? Not accumulated status, but the living value flowing from what was created.

**Stock**—money, shares, points—accumulates and concentrates. Whoever has more often sees their advantage grow on its own. Many stock-based systems tend toward concentration. The real world requires institutions that manually fight this centripetal pull.

**Flow**—value that is currently streaming from what was created. Flow fades when the contribution is no longer useful. It lives as long as it remains useful. Although access to sales channels and infrastructure can also concentrate flows, flow is not a panacea.

This system measures flow: not “how much you have accumulated,” but “how much value continues to flow from what you made.” It is possible that a flow model is less prone to concentration. But that still has to be tested in practice.

## What this looks like

This is not a job opening. Not an internship. It is a workshop—a place where a person completes their own finished thing alongside an engineer who is responsible for the whole. At the same time, there is an organizational framework here: boundaries, acceptance criteria, allocation of responsibility.

A fine line that we are trying to maintain.

You lead one product of your own — a **Werkstück**. Not “helping with various things,” but being responsible for something specific from start to finish. This may be:

- a design system for a specific type of project
- a methodology for auditing a business’s presence in AI systems
- a component library
- something else equally complete

In the workshop, everyone has their own workstation. Boundaries are named clearly. You work in your own space. You hand over a finished product in a form ready for use. This is the discipline of the workshop: the whole holds together when everyone knows their zone of responsibility.

When your product is accepted (the criteria must be in writing before the work begins), you receive an **authorship document**. Signed, with your name on it. This is a technical function of recording, an evidentiary trace. Not a magical substitute for a court or a contract. But it remains with you under any circumstances—even if the collaboration ends, even if the studio itself ends.

And most importantly: as long as the product is sold, you have a stake in it. In the current draft of the model, it looks like this: 30% of revenue in the first year, 10% in the second, 1% in perpetuity. These are working figures for the experiment. They need to be tested for economic viability.

## Honest risks

I acknowledge the asymmetry of risk. The author invests time and life without guarantees. The studio also spends resources—infrastructure, the engineer’s time, sales. But the author’s risk is more direct and more painful.

**Lack of baseline income.** The model does not replace social protection. A person leading a Werkstück often cannot simultaneously work full-time elsewhere. They end up in a zone of prolonged unpaid work without any guarantee that revenue will ever appear.

This filters participants by privilege. A financial cushion, a partner with income, savings… We risk selecting not the most capable, but those who can afford to wait. Without addressing this, we reproduce the class-based entry barrier we claim to resist.

**Legal risk in Germany.** In the draft agreement, I am looking in the direction of a Werkvertrag. But German regulators look at the actual indicators. Integration into the workshop, discipline, acceptance control—all this can be interpreted as concealed subordination (_Scheinselbständigkeit_). This has to be checked at the level of how the work is actually organized.

**Deferred revenue may never arrive.** Some Werkstücke may never bring in money. If 1 out of 10 sells, then 9 authors worked for free. That is not fraud if everything is stated honestly. But it is asymmetric risk.

**The difficulty of attribution for Wirkung.** How do you measure contribution to sales, support, client acquisition? Wirkung is the most vulnerable area for conflicts and a sense of unfairness. It requires shorter, more transparent, and more cautious mechanics than Werk.

**Dependence on the studio.** Even if the thing was verifiably created by you, its value depends entirely on sales, to which you may have no access. This is a structural asymmetry. The model does not yet fully solve it.

## What the model needs in order to evolve

Without minimum protections, the model will look like… romanticized unpaid labor. Here is what I see as the next necessary layers. Most of them are still only plans.

**Minimum stipend grant** _(planned)_. An interest-free grant (for example, €900–1,200/month for 3–6 months), which the author repays from the first 50% of future revenue. Or does not repay if no revenue appears. This is not a wage. It is the studio’s investment in the author and in their protection.

**Portfolio mutual-insurance fund** _(in draft)_. 10% of revenue from each successful product goes into a common fund. From it, a fixed payment is made for a completed but unsold Werk. This distributes risk across all authors.

**Two-sided fair buyout option** _(partly in draft)_. In the earlier logic, the weak point was a one-sided buyout. In the new version, I am trying to make it two-sided: the author can also initiate a buyout under the same formula. A multiplier of 24 is a working compromise. It needs testing.

**Guaranteed attempt to sell** _(planned)_. The studio commits, over a defined period, to making commercially reasonable efforts to sell the product. If there are no sales within a year, the author may take the Werkstück back. One of the main safeguards against dependence on the studio.

**Transparent reporting** _(partly implemented)_. The author must be able to see sales, revenue, returns. Without transparent reporting, revenue sharing does not inspire trust.

**Three honest exits** _(in development)_. The model should provide for three end scenarios: successful sale and payouts, return of the Werkstück to the author if it does not sell, buyout by either side under a fair formula. Each exit should be defined before the work begins.

## Who this may work for

The model is not suitable for everyone. I see its boundaries fairly clearly.

**Who this may suit:**

- Specialists with a finished portfolio who want to create a product of their own.
- Those who have a financial cushion for 3–6 months.
- People ready for entrepreneurial risk, but without capital for their own business.
- Authors of methodologies, systems, tools—things that can live a long time.

**Who this does not suit:**

- Beginners who need mentoring and training (most likely).
- People without a financial cushion.
- Those seeking stable income in the coming months.
- Specialists in fields where the result is difficult to separate from the process.

## Why this matters right now

My working hypothesis: the wage form is under pressure. Selling time is becoming a less reliable strategy. Especially in design and software development, where AI is beginning to automate routine tasks. This is not a fact. It is my sense of the trend.

If this hypothesis is correct, then when the wage form compresses opportunities, what remains will be capital (for those who have money) and the created (for those who have contribution). Right now I am trying to build infrastructure for the latter. Not as a universal replacement, but as a niche experiment for work that can become a finished asset with a long life.

The model is honest about its boundaries: “not a replacement for social protection,” “not suitable for quick income.” But it is precisely these boundaries that make it accessible only to those who already have financial security.

This is a contradiction that has not yet been resolved in practice.

Perhaps this will remain the small-scale practice of a single studio. That is also fine. But it seems important to try while there is still time for mistakes. Because if the anxiety about the future of wages proves justified, we will need new forms of participation. And it is better to experiment now than to wait until it becomes a necessity.

## The line between this and unpaid labor

The most important question I ask myself is this: how is this “workshop” different from unpaid collaboration with a nicer name?

The answer is still incomplete. What distinguishes the model from ordinary exploitation:

- Written acceptance criteria before the work begins.
- An authorship document that remains with the author.
- A guaranteed attempt to sell (planned).
- Transparent sales reporting.
- The right to take the Werkstück back if it does not sell (planned).
- A two-sided buyout option.
- A development grant (planned).
- A mutual-insurance fund (in draft).

But without baseline income, all of this can still become a trap for those who have no choice. That is why I am not presenting this as a finished solution. It is an experiment with honest boundaries.

This is not a final answer. But the question is worth asking: “Can there be a way to participate in what is created that requires neither selling time nor money upfront?” And if so: “How can it be made genuinely fair for all sides?”

## Quick self-audit: is the “participation through what is created” format right for you

This model is not about “yes/no,” and it is not a promise of income. But you can honestly check where the main risk lies for you: in your financial cushion, the type of contribution, dependence on the studio, or legal uncertainty. Answer a few questions, and you will get a short risk profile and a list of questions worth asking before you start.
