In web development, we usually discuss the stack, delivery, quality, and code ownership. But much less often, we discuss the form of participation in the value that arises after the work is delivered. It seems to me there is a fundamental distinction here, without which any conversation about a "share in the outcome" remains vague.

- Part of web work creates artifacts with their own state: a component library, design system, internal framework, modules, standardized information architecture, audit tools, documented patterns. These can be versioned, accepted against criteria, maintained, and reused. This kind of result is closer to a digital asset than to simply time spent.
- Another part is action within the process: infrastructure support, integration, advisory influence, release support, improving team productivity, participation in the sales loop. The value here is also unquestionable, but it exists less strongly as a separate object. Because of this, these are precisely the kinds of contributions that most often translate poorly into models of long-term participation in revenue or rights to future value.
- For me, the conclusion is practical: if a team wants to think seriously about ownership beyond salary, it first needs to classify types of contribution. Without that, there is no fair acceptance, no clear attribution, and no sound exit design. In web development, this is essentially a matter of informational and contractual architecture, not just compensation philosophy.

Which types of outcomes in web development do you consider genuine digital assets suitable for longer-term participation, and which would you keep within the bounds of classic process-based compensation?
