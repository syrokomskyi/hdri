In social media marketing, two different things are constantly being confused: a created asset and a useful effect over time. As long as they remain mixed together, conversations about long-term participation in the result almost always end either in resentment or in vagueness. And this is not a matter of work style, but a matter of structure.

- There are contributions that take the form of an artifact: a content system, a library of formats, editorial production standards, a structured methodology for brand presence, design components that can be reused across the team. These things can be described, transferred, accepted against criteria, and embedded into a brand’s or agency’s infrastructure as a separate object.
- There are contributions that exist as a dynamic effect: reach, engagement, maintaining the channel’s rhythm, audience response, impact on sales, moderation, operational discipline. All of this is very important, but it does not persist in the same way an artifact does. Here, the dependency on time, context, brand, team, platform, and external environment is too strong.
- So for me, the main question is not whether someone can be given a share of future value. The question is what exact type of contribution that would make sense for in the first place. If the structure is built around a dynamic effect, it often does not withstand attribution, a long time horizon, or scrutiny for fairness to both sides.

Which elements of social media work do you consider a true digital asset, and which are merely a temporary effect that should not be tied to long-term participation?
