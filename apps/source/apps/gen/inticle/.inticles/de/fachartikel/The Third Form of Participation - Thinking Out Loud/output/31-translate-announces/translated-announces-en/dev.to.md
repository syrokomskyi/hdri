I am writing about a participation model that sits between salary and equity, but cannot be reduced to either of these forms.

This is not about team motivation and not about yet another compensation scheme. It is about the architecture of contribution: when a stake arises from what a specialist has created as a completed digital object, rather than from mere presence or a financial investment.

The key to this construct is the distinction between Werk and Wirkung.

Werk is an artifact with its own state: a component library, a design system, a methodology, or another reproducible result. It can be documented, transferred, accepted against criteria, and embedded into a studio's infrastructure.

Wirkung is impact over time: sales, support, acquisition, operational effect. It is real, but it accumulates poorly as a separate asset and is much harder to attribute fairly.

In the article, I examine why this distinction matters for building a participation model in which the logic of authorship, responsibility, and accountability is not undermined. I also address the system's boundaries: legal interpretation, dependence on the sales channel, the conditions under which a Werkstück can be returned, and the risk of romanticized unpaid labor.

This is not a ready-made recipe. Rather, it is an attempt to describe a professional framework in which a digital product is understood as a carrier of long-term value, rather than as a one-off project output.
