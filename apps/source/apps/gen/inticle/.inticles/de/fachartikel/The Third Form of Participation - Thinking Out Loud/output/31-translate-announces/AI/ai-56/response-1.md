In professional environments, two forms of participation in economic value dominate.

The first is payment for time. The second is participation through capital.

But for some types of digital work, this is no longer sufficient. Especially where the result can be structured as a distinct reproducible asset.

In the new text, I examine a third construct: participation through what has been created. That is, through a Werkstück that has boundaries, acceptance criteria, authorship attribution, and the potential for a long commercial life.

The key distinction runs between Werk and Wirkung. Werk is a result with a defined state and structure. Wirkung is an effect that manifests only over time and is far more difficult to attribute in a stable way.

From an economic perspective, this matters. Not every contribution is equally suitable for long-term participation in revenue. If types of value are not distinguished, the compensation system quickly becomes a source of disputes.

I also examine the limits of the model separately. Its legal vulnerability. The asymmetry of risk. The Author’s dependence on sales infrastructure. The need for transparent reporting and predefined exit scenarios.

The position here is restrained. This is not a universal formula and not a replacement for traditional employment. It is a niche experiment for cases where what has been created can genuinely function as a digital asset.

Under current conditions, in which selling time is becoming a less stable model for some specialists, the question of participation infrastructure no longer appears purely theoretical.
