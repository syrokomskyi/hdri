There are two familiar forms of participation in economic value.

The first is wages. They are tied to time. The second is capital. It is tied to money invested upfront.

But between them, an almost empty space remains. A space where a person enters not with money and not simply with presence, but with a created result.

This is precisely the structure I examine in the new text. Not as a ready-made model, but as a working architecture with clearly defined boundaries.

At the center is the distinction between Werk and Wirkung. Between that which has form, state, and can exist as a separate asset, and that which operates over time but does not accumulate as an independent object.

This distinction is not a theoretical embellishment. It determines where participation can be justified in the long term, and where any "perpetual share" quickly turns into a source of conflict.

I also address uncomfortable questions. Legal vulnerability in Germany. The asymmetry of risk between the studio and the Author. Dependence on the sales channel. The line beyond which a "workshop" becomes merely a more refined name for unpaid labor.

There is no romanticization here. If a model lacks minimum protections, transparent reporting, the right to reclaim the Werkstück, and predefined exit mechanisms, it does not withstand ethical scrutiny.

It seems important to me to discuss not only the instruments of earning, but the very infrastructure of participation. Especially at a moment when selling time is becoming a less stable arrangement for part of the professional world.

The question is probably not whether such a model will replace traditional employment. The question is different: "Which forms of participation have any chance at all of being fair under the new conditions?"
