It seems to me that professional digital marketing is missing one basic conversation: not about channels, not about tools, but about the very nature of contribution. As long as everything is called a “result,” we cannot properly design either collaboration models, long-term incentives, or a fair allocation of risk.

- I see a fundamental difference between a created artifact and impact over time. An artifact is something that has boundaries, structure, acceptance criteria, can be reused, and continues to deliver value after the work is completed. In digital, these may include systems, libraries, methodologies, standards, and formalized processes. Impact over time is sales, acquisition, channel maintenance, operational action. It is real, but it does not exist as a separate stable object.
- This distinction matters not only in theory. It determines whether it is even possible to seriously discuss long-term participation in revenue. For an artifact, such a conversation still has grounds. For dynamic impact, it is far more vulnerable, because attribution is weaker there and dependence on the entire system is stronger: brand, sales, support, market, operations.
- From this follows a more uncomfortable conclusion. If these things are not named precisely, any “partnership” model risks becoming either unfair to the Contractor or economically unbearable for the company. The problem is usually not with the idea of participation as such, but with the fact that it is being applied to a type of contribution that is structurally unsuited to it.

In your view, where does the professionally honest boundary lie between digital contribution as an asset and digital contribution as a temporary effect?
