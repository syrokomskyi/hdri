In digital marketing, we far too easily call everything a "result," even though from a mechanical standpoint these are entirely different things. One is when a specialist creates a structured asset that can be reused. Another is when they produce an effect that exists only in the movement of the system. And this is exactly why compensation models so often break down.

- It is useful to distinguish at least two types of contribution. The first is artifact-based: a methodology, a templated process, a library of content or analytics components, an audit system, a standardized framework for presence in AI search. The second is dynamic: sales, campaign impact, demand generation, maintaining visibility, operational support. Both types are valuable, but they fundamentally persist in different ways once the work is finished.
- If an artifact has boundaries, acceptance criteria, and an evidentiary trail, then it is still possible to talk about participation in future revenue as a more or less structured model. But if we are talking about a dynamic effect, long-term participation quickly becomes unstable: too many variables, attribution is too weak, and the space for subjective interpretation is too large.
- So for me, the real question is not how to "motivate people with a share," but which types of digital contribution are suitable for such a structure in the first place. Without that, marketing language merely closes its eyes to weak agreement engineering.
