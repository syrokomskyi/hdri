In many companies, the website is treated as a necessary digital element.
But economically, it behaves like an expense.

The reason is often not technical.
It lies in the architecture of the solution.

If a website is not measured, is not under the owner’s full control, and does not accumulate effect over time, it remains a storefront rather than an asset.

In this article, I examine six conditions by which this can be assessed.
Among them: analytics, rights and access, organic accumulation of value, integration into operational processes, regular maintenance, and machine readability.

For the Mittelstand, this is especially important.
A website affects not only customer acquisition, but also the business’s digital independence, the reduction of Inhaberrisiko, and the quality of transferring the company to the next owner.

A separate issue is the contractor’s responsibility.
It is reasonable to promise technical reliability, transparent infrastructure, and support according to defined procedures.
It is not reasonable to promise economic results if the key variables lie outside the contractor’s zone of control.

Against this backdrop, AI is changing not fashion, but the requirements for information structure.
If a website is poorly readable by machines, the business loses part of its future visibility.

From a practical standpoint, it is useful to ask one question:
if paid promotion stops, will the website retain value for the company?
