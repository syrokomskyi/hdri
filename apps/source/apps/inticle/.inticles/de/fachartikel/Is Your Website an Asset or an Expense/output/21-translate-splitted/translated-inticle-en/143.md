## What it comes down to

I am not calling for websites to be urgently rebuilt or for expensive audits to be commissioned.

I am suggesting that you answer these questions honestly:

- Do you know how many people come to your website and what they do there?
- Could you move the website to another provider tomorrow?
- Is the website becoming more effective with each passing month, or does it stall without advertising?
- Does the website save time on routine tasks?
- Are the content and technologies being updated?
- Is the structure of the business understandable not only to people, but also to the search engines of the future?

The answers will show where you stand. It is perfectly normal if not all of these conditions are met. What matters is understanding what you are buying and what you expect from it.

The problem begins when you think you are investing in an asset, but are actually funding… just a storefront.

The difference is not in the price or the technologies. The difference is in the approach. Are you buying a thing, or building a system?

Most websites remain an expense not because they are poorly made. But because they were conceived from the outset as artifacts rather than as tools.

Change the approach. The same budget will produce a different result.

For the German Mittelstand (small and medium-sized businesses), with its focus on long-term resilience, this is a natural way of thinking. Transfer it from production processes to digital infrastructure.

A website is not a one-time purchase. It is a system that requires attention, development, and a clear understanding of what it is for. Do you have that?
